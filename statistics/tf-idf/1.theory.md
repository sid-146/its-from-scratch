# Intuition and Theoretical Foundation of TF-IDF

## 1.1 Problem in Text Representation

- Natural language text cannot be directly used by machine learning models because models require **numerical input**.
- Therefore, text must be converted into **numeric vectors** that represent the importance of words in documents.
- A naive approach is to represent documents using a **Bag-of-Words (BoW)** model.
- In the Bag-of-Words model:
   - Each document is represented as a vector.
   - Each dimension corresponds to a unique word in the vocabulary.
   - The value represents the **count of that word in the document**.

Example:

Document collection:

```
D1: "the cat sat on the mat"
D2: "the dog sat on the log"
```

Vocabulary:

```
[the, cat, sat, on, mat, dog, log]
```

Bag-of-Words representation:

| Document | the | cat | sat | on  | mat | dog | log |
| -------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1       | 2   | 1   | 1   | 1   | 1   | 0   | 0   |
| D2       | 2   | 0   | 1   | 1   | 0   | 1   | 1   |

### Limitation of Bag-of-Words

- Bag-of-Words treats **all words equally important**.
- However, many words appear very frequently across documents and **carry little information**.

Examples:

Common words such as:

- "the"
- "is"
- "on"
- "and"

These words appear in almost every document and therefore **do not help distinguish documents**.

Therefore, simple word counts do not capture **how informative a word is**.

---

## 1.2 Idea Behind TF-IDF

TF-IDF was developed to solve this limitation.

The central idea is:

A word should be important if:

- It appears **frequently in a document**
- But appears **rarely across other documents**

This idea produces two components:

1. **Term Frequency (TF)**
   Measures how often a word appears in a document.

2. **Inverse Document Frequency (IDF)**
   Measures how rare the word is across the document collection.

These two quantities are multiplied:

[
TF\text{-}IDF = TF \times IDF
]

A word receives a **high TF-IDF score** if:

- It appears many times in the document
- But appears in very few documents overall

A word receives a **low TF-IDF score** if:

- It appears in many documents
- Therefore carries little discriminative information.

---

## 1.3 Conceptual Intuition

TF-IDF balances **local importance** and **global rarity**.

Local importance:

- If a word appears many times in a document, it likely represents the **topic of the document**.

Global rarity:

- If the same word appears in almost every document, it is **not useful for distinguishing documents**.

Therefore TF-IDF performs a **weighting correction**.

Example intuition:

Document about **machine learning**

```
machine learning algorithms machine learning model training
```

Word frequencies:

| Word      | Frequency |
| --------- | --------- |
| machine   | high      |
| learning  | high      |
| algorithm | medium    |

But words like:

- "the"
- "is"
- "and"

would appear in many documents.

TF-IDF reduces their importance automatically.

---

## 1.4 Information Retrieval Motivation

TF-IDF originated in **Information Retrieval (IR)**.

In search engines:

- A user submits a **query**.
- The system must determine **which documents are most relevant**.

Example query:

```
deep learning optimization
```

A relevant document should:

- Contain these terms
- Preferably contain **rare informative terms**

TF-IDF allows documents to be ranked according to **term importance**.

This became the foundation of early search engines before neural embeddings.

---

## 1.5 Key Properties of TF-IDF

TF-IDF has several useful properties.

- It produces **numerical vectors** suitable for machine learning models.
- It automatically **downweights common words**.
- It highlights **topic-specific terms**.
- It works well for **large text collections**.
- It is computationally **simple and efficient**.

Because of these properties, TF-IDF is widely used in:

- document ranking
- text classification
- clustering
- keyword extraction
- classical information retrieval systems.

---

## 1.6 Conceptual Summary

TF-IDF assigns importance to words based on two principles:

Term Frequency:

- Words appearing frequently in a document are important for that document.

Inverse Document Frequency:

- Words appearing in many documents are less informative.

Combined idea:

- Important words are **frequent in a document but rare across the corpus**.
